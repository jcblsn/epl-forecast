# E001 — Initial results-based benchmarks

Run on 2026-09-05. The first milestone is reproducible with:

```sh
uv sync --locked
uv run python scripts/reproduce.py --output runs/reproduction
```

## Question and protocol

Do team attack/defense parameters improve prediction over league-only outcomes
and goal rates, without xG, players or bookmaker inputs?

Data: the pinned 2010/11–2025/26 PL and Championship archive has 14,912 complete
results. These models use PL results only. The Championship data are retained for
later continuity experiments. [Data audit](../data_inventory.md) and
[schema](../match_schema.md) describe the sources and checks.

All models fit before each date on the preceding 1,095 days. Goal models use a
365-day half-life. Model details and unoptimized starting parameters were fixed
before scoring. Development spans 2015/16–2022/23 (3,040 matches), validation
2023/24–2024/25 (760), and holdout 2025/26 (380). The
[selection record](../../configs/baseline_selection.json) precedes the holdout run.
The holdout is now observed and must not be reused as a fresh final test after tuning.

## Results

Lower log loss, Brier and score NLL are better. Brier sums over three outcomes.

| Model | Data used | Development log loss | Validation log loss | Holdout log loss | Holdout Brier | Holdout ECE | Holdout score NLL |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| M0 — league frequency | Prior PL H/D/A outcomes | 1.06530 | 1.06870 | 1.08371 | 0.65589 | 0.02728 | — |
| M1 — league Poisson | Prior PL scores | 1.06510 | 1.06806 | 1.08303 | 0.65565 | 0.01995 | 2.94923 |
| M2 — attack/defense Poisson | Prior PL scores and team IDs | 0.97176 | 0.95734 | 1.02649 | 0.61569 | 0.04505 | 2.89353 |
| Bet365 pre-closing comparator | Archived prices; external benchmark | 0.95797 | 0.93999 | 1.01855 | 0.61151 | 0.04221 | — |
| Market-average closing comparator | Archived prices; external benchmark | — | 0.93372 | 1.01177 | 0.60774 | 0.03614 | — |

Closing-market coverage begins in 2019/20, so it does not cover the full
development period; compare only the matched rows in the saved tables. Prices
have no individual collection timestamps and a different information horizon
from daily model forecasts. These are external comparisons, not same-horizon
claims of an achievable trading advantage.

M2 improves holdout log loss by 0.05721 relative to M0. The paired 28-day block
bootstrap interval for the difference, M2 minus M0, is [−0.07935, −0.02939]. The
validation difference is −0.11135 [−0.14409, −0.07924]. M2 also improves observed
score likelihood versus M1 in every aggregate split. These results support M2 as
the provisional structural baseline. They do not establish it as the best
possible team model.

M1's outcome-loss change versus M0 is negligible: development difference
−0.00020, interval [−0.00225, 0.00188]. Keep it as a score-generating control;
reject the claim that league-average Poisson materially improves H/D/A accuracy.

M2 still trails the market comparator. Its larger holdout ECE shows calibration
needs attention even though its proper scores improve over M0/M1. A constant
league baseline can be well calibrated in aggregate while failing to distinguish
strong and weak teams. Holdout draw probability averaged 23.55% against a 27.37%
observed draw rate; that is a diagnostic to investigate chronologically, not proof
that a particular draw correction will help.

## Season simulation demonstration

Using M2 at the start of 2025-01-01, simulate the 192 fixtures remaining in
2024/25 after fixing 188 completed results. Seed 20260905, 10,000 draws. This is a
retrospective demonstration of the pipeline, not a current-season forecast.

| Team | Mean final points | Title probability |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Liverpool | 86.12 | 83.32% |
| Arsenal | 78.21 | 15.45% |
| Manchester City | 69.87 | 0.89% |
| Chelsea | 65.72 | 0.20% |
| Newcastle United | 65.22 | 0.13% |

The [reference table](E001/simulation/table.csv) contains all 20 teams. Complete
position, points and goal-difference distributions are generated in
`runs/reproduction/simulation/simulation.json`. Title probabilities sum to 1,
relegation probabilities to 3, and top-four probabilities to 4. Every team's
position distribution and every position's team probabilities sum to 1.

The example European scenario assumes four league UCL places, Arsenal as FA Cup
winners and Liverpool as EFL Cup winners, with no extra English UEFA titleholders
or eligibility exclusions. It is hypothetical. Conditional qualification sums
are 4 UCL, 2 Europa League and 1 Conference League place. Head-to-head rules were
needed in 0.10% of draws; 0.01% retained a decisive tie handled using the explicit
equal-playoff-chance approximation. Simulation omits parameter uncertainty.

## Verification and reference artifacts

The complete reproduction was also run against a fresh raw-data directory. It
restored all 32 pinned files. Two offline normalizations and the original processed
dataset agreed byte-for-byte. All development, validation and holdout prediction
files, metrics and market forecasts matched the initial run exactly; the repeated
10,000-draw simulation was also byte-identical. The installed wheel includes the
team and sanction registries and exposes the CLI.

The separate pinned OpenFootball archive agrees on all 380 scores and dates in
2024/25. Its [comparison record](../crosscheck.json) includes the immutable commit
URL and checksum. This cross-check is limited to that season and cannot establish
that all historical source revisions were available in real time.

The test suite covers leakage, score likelihoods, numerical gradients, corrupted
raw data, missing/invalid fields, full fixture coverage, sanctions known at the
cutoff, tie rules, European pass-downs and probability conservation. Ruff and
the offline tests pass. The current limitations and rule assumptions are detailed
in [modeling conventions](../modeling.md).

## Next decisions

Do not add several information layers at once. The next research queue is in
[next experiments](../next_experiments.md). First broaden the team-level benchmark
comparison and inspect scoring/calibration errors; then evaluate promoted-team
continuity or lagged shot statistics. No authenticated source is required for
those first experiments. Live fixtures and richer data require a separate access
and coverage audit before implementation.
